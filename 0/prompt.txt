## Findings (most severe first)

1. **A GPT-Live reply mid-request splits the request in two.** Confirmed with a test. `lib/ai/voice-call-controller.ts:168` calls `claimUnheard()` (lines 209-217) as soon as GPT-Live's reply has 3 or more non-backchannel words.
   - **Scenario:** an earlier hand-off exists. The user says "I paid twenty euros", pauses, and GPT-Live says "Mm-hm. Take your time." The user goes on "for groceries at Rewe" and the hand-off arrives.
   - **What happens:** two requests start, "I paid twenty euros" and "for groceries at Rewe". The first one is answered through the old hand-off, so you get an incomplete card for 20 euros plus a broken second request.
   - **Cause:** "take your time" counts as a reply, and "paid" makes the half sentence look like a request. Your scenario "GPT-Live replying with a real sentence to half a request" hits exactly this.
   - **Fix:** only claim on a reply once the user has really finished, for example after `USER_PAUSE_MS` since their last word. Or start the claim only when GPT-Live says it is on it, and keep the 4 s fallback otherwise.

2. **A request can be lost when GPT-Live speaks before its hand-off arrives.** Confirmed in a unit test. Whether real calls send events in this order is unconfirmed. Code: lines 168 and 212, then 199-200.
   - **Scenario:** the user says "Rewe groceries twenty euros". GPT-Live says "Got it, making a card." and only then does the hand-off arrive.
   - **What happens:** no request runs at all. `looksLikeRequest` is false because none of the words are in its list, so `claimUnheard` drops the words as small talk. The hand-off then finds no words, waits 1.5 s and starts nothing.
   - **Fix:** don't throw words away on a reply alone. Keep them until a hand-off arrives or the 4 s quiet timer runs, and only drop them then.

3. **A late hand-off takes the next request's words, and answers go out under the wrong hand-off.** Confirmed with a test. Line 194: `wait ? this.heard : []`.
   - **Scenario:** hand-off d0 already exists. The user says "Check my rent" and GPT-Live replies "Sure, on that one too", so a request starts under d0. The user goes on "And Netto spend?" (heard after dA's offset). Then dA arrives late, then dB.
   - **What happens:** dA takes "And Netto spend?" because no words came before its offset and it falls back to all heard words. The rent request keeps d0, and dB finds no words and starts nothing. Every answer goes out under the wrong hand-off.
   - **Fix:** when `wait` is true and nothing came before the offset, claim nothing yet. Hand over `unclaimedTask` first, and use `this.heard` only for `offset === Infinity`. The same thing happens when `start_ms` and `end_ms` are both missing, because the word then gets `start = Infinity`.

4. **A correction doesn't silence a result the user cut off.** Confirmed with a test. `replaced()` (lines 346-351) only clears the queue, not `this.cut` or `this.current`.
   - **Scenario:** "Add coffee 5". The card result starts being spoken, and the user cuts in with "No, make it fifteen", which calls `call_replace_task` on r1.
   - **What happens:** after the reply, `resume()` (line 385) still asks GPT-Live to finish the old "Card for 5" result. That is exactly what item 3 of the PR is meant to stop.
   - **Fix:** in `replaced()`, clear `this.cut` if it belongs to that request. Storing `requestId` on `cut` and `current` would make the match straightforward.

5. **Hard-to-read code for the showcase.**
   - `claim()` line 194 packs three cases into one ternary. Finding 3 hides there.
   - The strip-tags-and-collapse-spaces regex appears three times (lines 196, 211, 220). One small `words(text)` helper would do.

   - `followUp` and `watchSlow` both re-check in loops that are hard to follow. A comment saying each one ends on its own would help.
   - Nothing on the controller stops its timers when the call ends. Tasks still in flight can keep handing results to a closed channel after hang-up. The timers do all end on their own, so this is minor and partly predates the PR.
